# DC4EU Business Blueprint: Comprehensive Recommendations for EU Member States

## Executive Summary

The DC4EU Business Blueprint establishes that successful implementation of digital credentials across the European Union requires coordinated national action that balances EU-wide interoperability with member state sovereignty in education and digital identity management. These recommendations provide systematic pathways for member states to align national digital strategies and legislative frameworks with eIDAS 2.0 requirements whilst facilitating comprehensive stakeholder adoption and cross-border interoperability.

The recommendations address the critical need for **"enhanced cooperation among stakeholders"** and **"universal frameworks"** identified in the Business Blueprint, whilst respecting that **"member states sovereignty"** in digital identity management must be preserved within the broader framework of EU digital integration.

## Strategic Context for Member State Implementation

### Balancing EU Integration with National Sovereignty

The DC4EU Business Blueprint demonstrates that successful digital credential implementation requires member states to balance **EU-wide interoperability objectives** with **preservation of national educational and administrative traditions**. This approach ensures that:

- **National frameworks maintain their distinctive characteristics** whilst gaining cross-border interoperability
- **Educational sovereignty is preserved** within broader EU digital integration objectives
- **Citizens benefit from enhanced mobility** without losing connection to national educational systems
- **Administrative efficiency is improved** whilst respecting national governance structures

### ELM as Enabling Semantic Infrastructure

The European Learning Model (ELM) serves as the **"common language for education"** that enables diverse national systems to communicate effectively. Rather than replacing national frameworks, ELM provides the **semantic backbone** that allows national qualification frameworks to interoperate across borders whilst maintaining their domestic characteristics and legal validity.

### Stakeholder Commitment and Implementation Imperative

The **83% stakeholder commitment demonstrated in DC4EU** creates an obligation for member states to provide systematic support for transitioning from pilot validation to production-scale services. This includes addressing the **42% conditional commitment** requiring resource support whilst leveraging the **35% unconditional commitment** as implementation leaders.

### National EAA Typology and Role Mapping

#### Comprehensive EAA Classification Framework

**Qualified Electronic Attestations of Attributes (QEAAs):**
- **Educational institutions**: Universities, colleges, VET providers issuing **formal qualifications with highest assurance levels**
- **Professional licensing bodies**: Organizations issuing **regulated professional qualifications** (medical, legal, engineering licenses)
- **National examination boards**: Bodies responsible for **national standardized assessments** and certifications
- **Government certification agencies**: National bodies issuing **official government qualifications** and professional certifications

**Public Electronic Attestations of Attributes (PubEAAs):**
- **Public education authorities**: Regional/national bodies issuing **official educational records** and transcripts
- **Government agencies**: Authorities issuing **professional licenses and permits** for regulated professions
- **Quality assurance agencies**: Bodies providing **institutional and programme accreditations**
- **Social security and employment agencies**: Bodies issuing **employment-related qualifications** and social benefits

**Standard Electronic Attestations of Attributes (EAAs):**
- **Private training providers**: Organizations offering **non-formal learning credentials** and professional development certificates
- **Industry associations**: Bodies issuing **sector-specific professional certifications** and skill validations
- **Educational technology platforms**: Providers of **digital badges and micro-credentials**
- **Professional development organizations**: Bodies offering **continuing education credentials** and skill certifications

#### National EAA Catalogue Extension

**Sectoral service catalogue development:**
- Extend **EU sectoral EAA catalogue to national level**, creating comprehensive service catalogues for education and professional qualifications
- Establish **national registries of authorised attributes** that relying parties can request based on sector and services offered
- Ensure all national catalogues comply with **GDPR data minimisation principles** and eIDAS 2.0 requirements

**Authorised attributes framework:**
- Define **specific attributes available for each EAA type** and service category
- Implement **regular review and update processes** for authorised attribute lists
- Establish **compliance monitoring and audit procedures** for attribute usage

## Conclusion

The successful implementation of digital credentials across EU member states requires coordinated national action that balances European integration objectives with respect for national sovereignty and educational traditions. These recommendations provide a comprehensive framework for member states to implement eIDAS 2.0, EUDIW, and ELM whilst preserving their distinctive national characteristics and governance structures.

The European Learning Model serves as the critical semantic infrastructure enabling diverse national systems to communicate effectively across borders. This approach ensures that educational credentials issued in one member state are semantically interpretable and verifiable across all EU digital services, supporting both European integration and the fundamental principle of subsidiarity in education.

Member states must recognise that digital credential infrastructure represents critical infrastructure for national competitiveness and citizen empowerment in the digital age. The transformation from traditional paper-based systems to digital credential infrastructure offers significant opportunities for administrative efficiency, citizen service enhancement, and economic development.

Through systematic implementation of these recommendations, member states can contribute to establishing European leadership in digital credentials whilst serving the practical needs of their citizens seeking education, employment, and mobility opportunities. Success requires sustained political commitment, adequate resource allocation, and recognition that digital credential implementation represents a fundamental enhancement of democratic governance and citizen empowerment in the 21st century.

The exceptional stakeholder commitment demonstrated through DC4EU creates both an opportunity and an obligation for member states to support the transition from pilot validation to production-scale services. By working together within the framework provided by these recommendations, member states can ensure that Europe remains at the forefront of global digital transformation whilst preserving the educational and cultural diversity that strengthens European society.
