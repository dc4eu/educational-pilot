# DC4EU Business Blueprint: Comprehensive Recommendations for European Commission Directorate-Generals

## Executive Summary

The DC4EU Business Blueprint represents a transformative framework for implementing digital credentials across the European Union's educational and professional qualification systems. Based on comprehensive analysis of stakeholder engagement, technical validation, and cross-border implementation requirements, this document presents strategic recommendations for European Commission Directorate-Generals to ensure successful adoption of eIDAS 2.0, European Digital Identity Wallet (EUDIW), and the European Learning Model (ELM) as foundational infrastructure for Europe's digital credential ecosystem.

The recommendations address critical coordination gaps, leverage the substantial stakeholder commitment demonstrated through DC4EU (with 83% of partners expressing willingness to continue), and provide systematic pathways for transitioning from pilot validation to production-scale services that serve over 450 million EU citizens.

## Strategic Context and Foundation

### The European Learning Model as Semantic Infrastructure

The DC4EU Business Blueprint establishes the European Learning Model (ELM) as the **"common language for education"** across Europe. Just as the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) created a common currency for academic credits, ELM provides a unified semantic foundation that enables diverse national educational systems to communicate effectively whilst preserving their distinctive characteristics.

ELM serves as the **semantic backbone** that allows national frameworks to interoperate cross-border whilst maintaining domestic autonomy. This approach ensures that educational credentials issued in one member state are semantically interpretable and verifiable across all EU digital services, supporting the fundamental principle of subsidiarity in education whilst enabling European integration.

### Stakeholder Commitment and Market Validation

The exceptional **83% stakeholder commitment to continue DC4EU implementation** represents unprecedented validation of the digital credential value proposition. This includes:

- **35% unconditional commitment**: Stakeholders ready to implement production services with their own resources
- **42% conditional commitment**: Stakeholders requiring support for resource constraints but committed to implementation
- **8% uncommitted**: Minimal stakeholder uncertainty indicating broad consensus on value

This level of commitment creates both an opportunity and an obligation for the European Commission to provide systematic support for transitioning from pilot validation to production-scale services.

### eIDAS 2.0 Integration Imperative

The Business Blueprint demonstrates that eIDAS 2.0 compliance and EUDIW integration must be embedded as **mandatory requirements** rather than optional considerations in all relevant EU initiatives. The analysis reveals critical coordination gaps where educational and digital initiatives proceed without systematic eIDAS integration, creating fragmentation and reducing the effectiveness of EU digital transformation investments.

## DG-EAC Recommendations

### Educational Framework Integration and ELM Adoption

As the Directorate-General responsible for education across all levels, DG-EAC must lead the integration of digital credentials into European educational policy whilst ensuring ELM adoption as the common semantic framework.

#### Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary Education Alignment

**ELM curriculum integration:**
- Promote ELM adoption across all educational levels as the semantic foundation for cross-border credential recognition

**Technical and operational implementation:**
- Support the evolution of ELM v.3 with enhanced verifiable credentials properties and better claims management
- Fund development of ELM2ELMO, ELM2OpenBadges, and ELM4MicroCredentials converters to ensure semantic interoperability
- Embed ELM semantic standards into European quality assurance frameworks (ENQA, EQAR)

#### Educational Mobility Enhancement

**Student mobility facilitation:**
- Implement systems supporting seamless academic recognition across borders through digital credentials
- Develop VCs4OLA and VCs4EWP elements to enhance Erasmus Without Papers and Online Learning Agreement platforms
- Simplify processes for international academic recognition and credit transfer

**Lifelong learning support:**
- Develop systems supporting lifelong learning portfolio management
- Facilitate recognition and portability of micro-credentials across educational systems
- Implement mechanisms for tracking and verifying continuous professional development

#### Cultural and Linguistic Inclusivity

**Multilingual system development:**
- Ensure digital credential systems serve all EU citizens comprehensively across languages
- Develop systems respecting diverse educational traditions whilst maintaining interoperability
- Remove barriers for marginalised and underserved communities in accessing digital credentials

**Grant requirements:**
- Make eIDAS 2.0 compliance and DC4EU alignment explicit prerequisites for all educational funding programmes
- Include eIDAS integration as a mandatory scoring criterion in all educational project assessments
- Require existing projects entering new phases to demonstrate eIDAS compatibility or provide transition plans

## DG-EMPL Recommendations

### ELM Governance Framework and VET Integration

As the owner of ELM and manager of Europass, DG-EMPL must establish comprehensive governance frameworks ensuring ELM serves as the semantic backbone for all European digital credential initiatives whilst addressing employment and social inclusion objectives.

#### Multi-Level ELM Governance Framework

**Assembly of Members (Supreme Governing Body):**
- Representatives from all participating DGs, Member State education ministries, and key stakeholder organisations
- Key decisions on ELM strategic direction, major version approvals, budget allocation, and policy framework adoption
- Composition: DG-EMPL (Chair), DG-EAC, DG-GROW, DG-FIN, DG-CNECT, DG-DIGIT, plus Member State representatives

**ELM Director and Secretariat:**
- Day-to-day management and coordination of ELM development and implementation
- Technical coordination, stakeholder liaison, implementation monitoring, and strategic planning
- Location within DG-EMPL structure with dedicated resources and authority

**Strategic and Technical Advisory Bodies:**

*ELM Strategic Orientation Board:*
- European Commission representatives from all participating DGs
- Member State representatives: Education ministry officials with technical expertise
- Key stakeholder delegates: EMREX Board representative, GEANT, educational quality assurance bodies
- Expert advisors: Technical and policy experts in digital credentials and educational standards

*ELM Technical Group:*
- Technical experts appointed by DGs and Member States
- Standards specialists from educational technology and semantic interoperability domains
- Implementation practitioners from pilot projects and operational systems

#### VET and Adult Education Semantic Alignment

**Europass-ELM integration:**
- Strengthen the connection between ELM and Europass platform to ensure seamless credential portability
- Accelerate adoption of ELM v.3 as the standard for vocational education and training credentials
- Use ELM semantic framework to improve visibility and recognition of skills across EU labour markets

**Cross-border employability support:**
- Develop comprehensive semantic mappings between national professional qualification frameworks and ELM
- Implement ELM as the semantic backbone for EU micro-credentials, enabling local adaptations whilst ensuring cross-border recognition
- Guide the evolution of European Digital Credentials for Learners Issuer (EDCI) to fully leverage ELM semantic capabilities

#### Financial Governance and Sustainability

**Multi-DG financial framework:**
- DG-EMPL: Core ELM development and maintenance
- DG-EAC: Educational integration and quality assurance alignment
- DG-GROW: Single Digital Gateway integration and converter maintenance
- DG-CNECT: Technical infrastructure and digital innovation support
- DG-DIGIT: EBSI integration and technical platform support

**Member State contribution model:**
- Co-financing arrangements: Member States contribute to implementation costs proportional to benefits received
- Technical cooperation: Member States provide expertise and pilot project participation
- Infrastructure sharing: Leverage existing national technical infrastructure where appropriate

## DG-GROW Recommendations

### Single Digital Market Integration and Converter Sustainability

As the Directorate-General responsible for the Single Digital Gateway and Once Only Technical System, DG-GROW must ensure systematic integration of ELM semantic standards whilst assuming responsibility for critical technical infrastructure.

#### ELM-Based Standardisation and Converter Maintenance

**Standardisation track alignment with ELM ontology:**
- Establish ELM v.3 as the official ontological standard for all eIDAS-related educational data models within the Single Digital Gateway ecosystem
- Align all cross-border digital service data models with ELM's unified ontology
- Adopt ELM's JSON-LD implementation as the technical standard for semantic interoperability across all member state digital services

**DC4EU converter ecosystem adoption:**
The Business Blueprint identifies three critical converters developed under EUPLv2 license:
- **ELM2ELMO (bidirectional)**: Converting between European Learning Model and ELMO formats
- **ELM2OpenBadges (bidirectional)**: Converting between ELM and OpenBadges 3.0
- **ELM4MicroCredentials (bidirectional)**: Converting between ELM and EU micro-credential standards

**Immediate adoption strategy:**
- Establish formal procedures for transferring DC4EU converter intellectual property and technical assets to DG-GROW stewardship
- Secure comprehensive technical documentation, including semantic mapping rules, API specifications, and integration guidelines
- Ensure all converter source code is properly transferred under EUPLv2 license with clear maintenance and evolution rights

#### Single Digital Gateway Enhancement

**ELM-based service catalogues:**
- Implement ELM semantic frameworks within Single Digital Gateway to enable automatic cross-border recognition of educational qualifications and professional credentials
- Use ELM ontology to ensure that educational credentials submitted in one member state are semantically interpretable across all EU digital services
- Develop ELM-based sectoral catalogues that define educational and professional attributes required for different cross-border services

**Once Only Technical System integration:**
- Use ELM semantic standards to reduce administrative burden by enabling automatic credential translation and recognition
- Leverage ELM to streamline professional qualification verification for business establishment across member states
- Provide tools and guidance for small and medium enterprises to understand and verify ELM-based credentials

#### Sustainable Maintenance Framework

**Converter maintenance unit:**
- Establish dedicated technical team within DG-GROW or contracted services for ongoing converter maintenance and evolution
- Implement systematic version control ensuring converters remain aligned with evolving ELM ontology and external standards
- Develop comprehensive testing frameworks ensuring "loss-free conversion" as established in DC4EU specifications

**Financial sustainability model:**
- Establish sustainable funding model for converter maintenance, evolution, and support services
- Develop cost-sharing frameworks with member states and institutional users benefiting from converter services
- Explore sustainable revenue models through premium support services whilst maintaining core functionality as public good

## Member State Recommendations

### Comprehensive National Implementation Framework

Member States must align national digital strategies and legislative frameworks with eIDAS 2.0 requirements whilst facilitating comprehensive stakeholder adoption and cross-border interoperability.

#### eIDAS and SDG/OOTS Alignment Framework

**Technical integration requirements:**
- Integrate national business registers and educational credential systems with SDG infrastructure using eIDAS-compliant authentication mechanisms
- Ensure national systems can automatically exchange educational and professional credentials using ELM semantic frameworks
- Implement seamless data exchange between national systems and the Single Digital Gateway through standardised APIs

**Regulatory harmonisation:**
- Align national digital identity legislation with eIDAS 2.0 requirements, particularly for Electronic Attestations of Attributes (EAAs)
- Ensure national digital identity schemes are fully compatible with European Digital Identity Wallet architecture
- Harmonise national trust service frameworks with eIDAS 2.0 qualified trust service requirements

#### ELM Adoption in Cross-Border Context

**Semantic interoperability implementation:**
- Adopt European Learning Model v.3 as the standard semantic framework for all cross-border educational credential exchange
- Implement DC4EU-developed converters (ELM2ELMO, ELM2OpenBadges, ELM4MicroCredentials) to bridge national data models with ELM
- Use ELM semantic standards to enable automatic recognition of qualifications across member states

**National adaptation strategy:**
- Develop national ELM profiles that respect local educational traditions whilst ensuring European semantic compatibility
- Create comprehensive semantic mappings between national qualification frameworks and ELM ontology without loss of national specificities
- Launch coordinated pilot programmes across multiple member states to validate ELM cross-border interoperability

#### National EAA Catalogue Extension

**Sectoral service catalogue development:**
- Extend the EU sectoral EAA catalogue to national level, creating comprehensive service catalogues for education and professional qualifications
- Establish national registries of authorised attributes that relying parties can request based on sector and services offered
- Ensure all national catalogues comply with GDPR data minimisation principles and eIDAS 2.0 requirements

#### Stakeholder Engagement and EAA Ecosystem Development

**Structured stakeholder dialogue framework:**
- Establish permanent committee within education ministry responsible for EAA strategy development and implementation oversight
- Create formal coordination mechanisms between education, digital affairs, and justice ministries for coherent EAA policy development
- Engage Conference of Rectors/University associations in defining academic credential EAA requirements and implementation strategies

**National EAA typology and role mapping:**

*Qualified Electronic Attestations of Attributes (QEAAs):*
- Educational institutions: Universities, colleges, VET providers issuing formal qualifications with highest assurance levels
- Professional licensing bodies: Organizations issuing regulated professional qualifications
- National examination boards: Bodies responsible for national standardized assessments and certifications

*Public Electronic Attestations of Attributes (PubEAAs):*
- Public education authorities: Regional/national bodies issuing official educational records and transcripts
- Government agencies: Authorities issuing professional licenses and permits for regulated professions
- Quality assurance agencies: Bodies providing institutional and programme accreditations

*Standard Electronic Attestations of Attributes (EAAs):*
- Private training providers: Organizations offering non-formal learning credentials
- Industry associations: Bodies issuing sector-specific professional certifications
- Educational technology platforms: Providers of digital badges and micro-credentials

#### Legislative Framework Comprehensive Review

**Primary legislation assessment:**
- Ensure constitutional frameworks support digital identity and credential systems with appropriate privacy and rights protections
- Review and update national digital identity laws for full eIDAS 2.0 compliance and EUDIW integration
- Verify national data protection legislation compatibility with eIDAS 2.0 EAA processing requirements

**Sectoral legislation review:**
- Update national education laws to explicitly recognize and regulate EAAs across all educational levels
- Modify professional licensing and qualification legislation to include EAA recognition and verification procedures
- Ensure public service legislation supports EAA-based service delivery and verification processes

**EAA integration across legal dimensions:**
- Establish clear legal provisions ensuring EAAs have equivalent legal standing to traditional paper-based credentials
- Define EAA evidentiary value in legal proceedings and administrative processes
- Ensure legal prohibition of discrimination against EAA holders in favor of traditional credential formats

## Conclusion

The DC4EU Business Blueprint represents a comprehensive framework for transforming European education and professional qualification systems through digital credentials. The exceptional stakeholder commitment demonstrated through the pilot phase creates both an opportunity and an obligation for systematic support for production-scale implementation.

The recommendations presented in this document provide coordinated pathways for all relevant Directorate-Generals to work together in implementing eIDAS 2.0, EUDIW, and ELM as foundational infrastructure for Europe's digital future. Success requires sustained commitment, coordinated action, and recognition that digital credential infrastructure represents critical infrastructure for European competitiveness and citizen empowerment in the digital age.

The European Learning Model serves as the semantic backbone enabling diverse national systems to communicate effectively whilst preserving their distinctive characteristics. This approach ensures that educational credentials issued in one member state are semantically interpretable and verifiable across all EU digital services, supporting both European integration and the fundamental principle of subsidiarity in education.

Through systematic implementation of these recommendations, the European Union can establish global leadership in digital credentials whilst serving the practical needs of over 450 million citizens seeking education, employment, and mobility across European borders. The transformation from traditional paper-based systems to digital credential infrastructure represents not merely a technological upgrade, but a fundamental enhancement of European democratic values, individual empowerment, and economic competitiveness in the global digital economy.
