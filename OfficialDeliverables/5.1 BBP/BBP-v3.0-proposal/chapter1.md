## <a id="_Toc182376645"></a><a id="_Toc184709993"></a>Chapter 1: Introduction

<a id="_Toc182376646"></a>The European education system faces a significant change in how academic and professional qualifications are managed. As students and professionals become more mobile, the need for trusted digital credentials grows. This chapter outlines a comprehensive approach to digital credentials that serves the entire European education community.

### <a id="_Toc184709994"></a>1.1 Building a new approach to credentials

Digital credentials represent a step forward in how we handle qualifications across Europe. When a student completes their studies at a university in one European country and applies for a job in another, their qualifications need to be trusted and easily verified. Our blueprint shows how this can work.

The education sector needs common rules for creating and managing digital versions of qualifications. These rules ensure that a digital degree carries the same trust as a traditional paper diploma. A university in Spain issuing a digital degree needs to know that employers and universities in Germany or Poland will recognise and trust that credential.

Digital verification removes barriers to mobility. Rather than waiting weeks for paper documents to be checked and translated, organisations can verify qualifications immediately. This makes it easier for people to study and work across Europe while maintaining the high standards that education requires.

Educational institutions and professional bodies need practical guidance to make this change. The blueprint provides step-by-step instructions for moving from paper to digital credentials. It explains what systems institutions need and how they can work together across borders.

### <a id="_Toc184709995"></a>1.2 Creating trust in digital education

Trust in educational credentials comes from clear rules and reliable processes. A medical student graduating in France needs their digital qualification to be accepted by hospitals in Sweden or medical authorities in Italy. This trust stems from common standards in how credentials are created, shared, and checked.

Digital credentials change how educational organisations work together. A university can share student records securely with another institution. Professional bodies can check qualifications instantly. Employers can verify degrees without lengthy correspondence. These connections rely on clear agreements about who can do what within the system.

Personal information in educational records needs protection. When a student shares their degree with a potential employer, they should control exactly what information is shared. The system protects privacy while making verification simple. Students choose when to share their credentials, and organisations can check only what they need to see.

Moving qualifications across borders becomes straightforward. A degree earned in one country can be understood and trusted in another because the digital credential includes standardised information. This helps students continue their studies in different countries and professionals practice their careers across Europe.

### <a id="_Toc184709996"></a>1.3 Supporting the education community

#### Educational institution types and their needs:

**Universities and colleges**
- Face challenges with managing thousands of student records annually
- Need to transition from paper-based degree and transcript issuance
- Require instant digital sharing capabilities with employers and other institutions
- Benefit from streamlined graduation processes

**Professional training organisations**
- Provide specialised qualifications with detailed skill specifications
- Need to demonstrate clear connections between learning outcomes and professional standards
- Require credentials that clearly communicate competency levels to employers
- Focus on practical skills and knowledge application

**Schools and secondary education**
- Need efficient systems for recording student achievements
- Require seamless pathways for students transitioning to higher education or employment
- Benefit from comprehensive digital records that follow students throughout their educational journey
- Focus on foundational qualifications and career preparation

### <a id="_Toc184709997"></a>1.4 Connecting with Europe's Digital Future

#### European digital transformation benefits:

| Initiative | Current challenge | Digital credential solution |
|------------|------------------|----------------------------|
| **European Education Area** | Barriers between national education systems | Instant qualification verification across borders |
| **Cross-border mobility** | Complex paperwork and translation requirements | Immediate proof of qualifications in standardised format |
| **Qualification frameworks** | Different national description methods | Common European reference points and terminology |
| **Lifelong learning** | Fragmented record keeping across institutions | Comprehensive digital wallet for all achievements |
| **Online education** | Lack of trusted verification methods | Secure technology with clear standards for digital achievements |

The European Union aims to make education more accessible across borders. Our approach to digital credentials supports these plans by addressing each of these key areas systematically.

Digital credentials help deliver the promise of the European Education Area. A student with a bachelor's degree from Greece can apply to a master's programme in Denmark without waiting weeks for paperwork. Their digital credentials provide immediate proof of their qualifications.

Learning now happens throughout life, not just in traditional education. People take courses, gain certificates, and develop new skills continuously. Digital credentials create a complete record of these achievements. Someone might have a university degree, professional certifications, and specialised training – all stored securely in their digital wallet.

### <a id="_Toc184709998"></a>1.5 Making a difference

Digital credentials change how people manage their education and careers:

**For students and working professionals**, control over educational records becomes simpler. Their digital wallet holds all their qualifications securely. When applying for jobs or further study, they choose what to share. The system protects their privacy while making it easy to prove their achievements.

**Educational institutions** gain new efficiency. Issuing and checking qualifications becomes faster and more reliable. Instead of processing paper documents, they can verify credentials instantly. This saves time and money while improving service to students.

**The broader European community** benefits as people can move more easily between countries and institutions. A digital credential proven valid in one country works across Europe. This helps people study where they choose and work where they are needed.

### <a id="_Toc184709999"></a>1.6 Managing changes to qualifications

Sometimes qualifications need to be changed or withdrawn. Different European countries handle this in different ways, and our system adapts to these national approaches:

#### Qualification status management:

**Fraud prevention and cancellation**
- Digital system enables immediate qualification cancellation when fraud is discovered
- Instant visibility of cancellation status protects the value of genuine qualifications
- Clear processes ensure appropriate action while maintaining due process

**National regulatory variation**
- Some countries implement strict legal processes for qualification changes
- Others delegate more authority to educational institutions
- Digital system accommodates different approaches while maintaining European interoperability

**Temporary suspension procedures**
- Available in countries where national laws permit qualification suspension
- Used during investigations into serious concerns about qualification validity
- Clear status indication protects both individual rights and system integrity

### <a id="_Toc184710000"></a>1.7 Building connections

#### Institutional collaboration through digital credentials:

**Educational institution partnerships**
- **Student transfers**: Digital credentials show complete study history for instant verification
- **Credit recognition**: Automated assessment of course equivalencies  
- **Quality assurance**: Clear documentation of institutional standards and accreditation

**Professional-education alignment**
- **Standards mapping**: Direct connection between qualifications and professional requirements
- **Skills verification**: Clear demonstration of competency achievement
- **Career pathways**: Transparent progression routes from education to professional practice

**Employer integration**
- **Instant verification**: Real-time qualification checking for recruitment processes
- **Skills matching**: Precise alignment between qualifications and job requirements
- **Fraud prevention**: Secure, tamper-proof credential verification

### <a id="_Toc184710001"></a>1.8 Running the system

#### Organisational framework:

**Institutional level**
- Universities and professional bodies maintain direct control over their credentials
- Implementation follows common standards while preserving institutional independence
- Credentials designed for pan-European recognition and functionality

**National level**
- Countries retain sovereignty over education and professional qualification rules
- Digital system operates within existing national regulatory frameworks
- Cross-border recognition enabled through standardised approaches

**European level**
- Coordination ensures common standards and cross-border functionality
- Regular system monitoring maintains effectiveness and user satisfaction
- Continuous improvement based on stakeholder feedback and technological advancement

**Security and privacy principles**
- Personal information protection throughout all system operations
- Robust fraud prevention through advanced security features
- Balanced approach ensuring both privacy protection and verification capabilities
