# Annex D: Regulatory references

This annex provides a comprehensive overview of the key European regulations, directives, and frameworks that underpin the digital credential ecosystem for education and professional qualifications. These regulatory foundations ensure legal compliance, cross-border recognition, and alignment with broader European digital transformation objectives.

## Core regulatory framework

### 1. eIDAS Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 and Amendment (EU) 2024/1183

**Electronic Identification, Authentication and Trust Services Regulation**

This regulation provides the legal framework for electronic identification, authentication, and trust services in electronic transactions across the EU. The 2024 amendment establishes the European Digital Identity Framework (EUDI), which includes electronic attestations of attributes for credentials. It facilitates the legal recognition of digital credentials across Member States, supporting secure, interoperable credentialing.

**Key provisions for educational credentials:**
- Legal framework for electronic attestations of attributes (EAAs)
- Cross-border recognition of digital identities
- Trust service provider requirements
- Technical standards for interoperability

**Implementation implications:**
- Enables legal validity of digital educational credentials
- Supports the technical framework detailed in [Chapter 8](chapter8.md)
- Underpins the trust model described in [Section 4.1](chapter4.md#41-trust-model-and-governance-framework)

### 2. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (EU) 2016/679

**Data Protection and Privacy Regulation**

GDPR governs data protection and privacy within the EU, ensuring the rights of individuals to control their personal data. This regulation is crucial for digital credentialing, setting requirements for data security, consent, and lawful data processing, especially in cross-border educational and professional credential exchanges.

**Key requirements for credential systems:**
- Data minimisation principles
- Individual consent mechanisms
- Right to data portability
- Privacy by design implementation

**Framework alignment:**
- Supports the privacy-preserving features outlined in [Section 6.9](chapter6.md#69-selective-disclosure)
- Underpins the personal data protection measures in [Section 6.1](chapter6.md#61-natural-persons-identity)
- Ensures compliance with privacy requirements detailed in [Chapter 4](chapter4.md)

## Digital transformation framework

### 3. Single Digital Gateway (SDG) Regulation (EU) 2018/1724

**Digital Public Services Regulation**

The SDG regulation aims to simplify access to public services and administrative information across Member States. It supports the Once-Only Principle (OOP), ensuring that data collected from citizens is reused, reducing administrative burdens and promoting interoperability for credentials in education and employment.

**Benefits for credential management:**
- Reduces administrative duplication
- Enables cross-border service delivery
- Supports digital-first approaches
- Facilitates data reuse across services

**Connection to framework:**
- Aligns with the efficiency benefits described in [Section 4.6](chapter4.md#46-benefits-of-the-operational-model)
- Supports the cross-border use cases in [Chapter 7](chapter7.md)

### 4. European Digital Identity Framework (EUDI)

**Comprehensive Digital Identity System**

The EUDI framework is a comprehensive system for digital identity, enabling citizens to store and share verified personal attributes and credentials in a secure, standardised way. It supports the use of EUDI wallets, enhancing the portability of credentials and ensuring that digital identities are recognised across the EU.

**Core components:**
- European Digital Identity Wallets
- Qualified electronic attestation of attributes
- Cross-border identity verification
- Standardised attribute schemas

**Framework integration:**
- Central to the use cases demonstrated in [Chapter 7](chapter7.md)
- Enables the wallet-based credential management described throughout the document
- Supports the onboarding processes detailed in [Chapter 5](chapter5.md)

## Educational and qualification standards

### 5. European Qualifications Framework (EQF) Recommendation (2008/C 111/01)

**Common Qualification Reference Framework**

The EQF provides a common reference framework for comparing qualifications across Europe. This supports transparency, comparability, and the recognition of skills and qualifications, contributing to a more integrated European education and employment landscape.

**Key features:**
- Eight-level qualification structure
- Learning outcomes-based approach
- National qualification framework alignment
- Cross-border qualification comparison

**Implementation support:**
- Integrated into the data models described in [Chapter 9](chapter9.md) and [Annex C](annexes/AnnexC/README.md)
- Supports the recognition processes outlined in [Section 3.2](chapter3.md#32-recognition-of-qualifications)
- Enables the cross-border mobility scenarios in [Chapter 7](chapter7.md)

### 6. Professional Qualifications Directive (PQD) 2005/36/EC and Amendments

**Professional Recognition Framework**

This directive supports the recognition of professional qualifications across the EU, allowing professionals to work in other Member States. The directive aligns with the European Digital Identity and credentialing frameworks, ensuring that qualifications are recognised legally and efficiently.

**Scope and application:**
- Automatic recognition for specific professions
- General system for professional recognition
- Temporary mobility provisions
- Professional qualification requirements

**Digital credential relevance:**
- Supports the professional qualification use cases in [Section 7.7.3](chapter7.md#773-professional-qualifications)
- Enables the professional onboarding processes in [Section 5.2](chapter5.md#52-professional-qualifications-onboarding-process)

### 7. Europass Decision (Decision 2241/2004/EC)

**Skills and Qualifications Transparency Initiative**

Europass is an EU initiative for transparency in skills and qualifications, making it easier for citizens to present their skills across Europe. The updated Europass Digital Credentials Infrastructure (EDCI) aligns with the European Digital Identity to facilitate the issue and verification of digital credentials.

**Digital infrastructure:**
- Europass Digital Credentials Infrastructure (EDCI)
- European Learning Model implementation
- Multilingual credential support
- Cross-border recognition tools

**Technical alignment:**
- Implements the European Learning Model detailed in [Section 6.7](chapter6.md#67-education-and-professional-qualifications-ontology---european-learning-model-elm)
- Supports the data models described in [Annex C](annexes/AnnexC/README.md)

## Strategic initiatives

### 8. European Skills Agenda

**Upskilling and Reskilling Strategy**

The European Skills Agenda sets objectives for upskilling and reskilling across the EU, supporting lifelong learning and workforce mobility. It promotes recognition of micro-credentials and supports initiatives like Europass and ESCO for skill classification, in alignment with digital identity frameworks.

**Key objectives:**
- Lifelong learning promotion
- Micro-credential recognition
- Skills classification standardisation
- Workforce mobility enhancement

**Framework support:**
- Enables the lifelong learning scenarios described in [Chapter 7](chapter7.md)
- Supports the diverse credential types in the technical framework

### 9. Digital Education Action Plan (DEAP) 2021-2027

**Educational Digital Transformation Strategy**

The DEAP outlines actions to advance digital literacy and the use of digital technologies in education. It supports the development of high-quality digital education content and skills, and the digital transformation of educational institutions, promoting compatibility with European digital credentials.

**Priority areas:**
- Digital education infrastructure
- Digital skills development
- High-quality digital content
- Institutional digital transformation

**Implementation alignment:**
- Supports the institutional onboarding described in [Chapter 5](chapter5.md)
- Enables the educational transformation outlined in [Chapter 1](chapter1.md)

### 10. European Education Area (EEA) Initiative

**Barrier-Free European Education by 2025**

The EEA aims to remove barriers to studying and working across Europe by 2025, promoting qualification portability and mobility. Trust frameworks for digital credentials are a key element, as they support seamless recognition of qualifications across borders.

**Core objectives:**
- Cross-border education mobility
- Qualification portability
- Quality assurance alignment
- Mutual recognition enhancement

**Digital credential role:**
- Fundamental to achieving EEA objectives
- Enabled by the framework described in [Chapter 4](chapter4.md)
- Demonstrated through use cases in [Chapter 7](chapter7.md)

## Technology and data strategies

### 11. European Blockchain Strategy

**Trusted Digital Services through Blockchain**

Part of the European Digital Strategy, this strategy focuses on using blockchain technology to support trusted and secure services. The European Blockchain Services Infrastructure (EBSI) aligns with this strategy, providing a foundation for verifiable digital credentials and promoting interoperability in education and employment.

**Technical components:**
- European Blockchain Services Infrastructure (EBSI)
- Verifiable credential anchoring
- Tamper-proof record keeping
- Cross-border interoperability

**Framework integration:**
- Supports the trust infrastructure described in [Section 4.1](chapter4.md#41-trust-model-and-governance-framework)
- Enables the verification processes detailed in [Section 6.11](chapter6.md#611-verification)

### 12. European Data Strategy

**Data Sovereignty and Controlled Sharing**

This strategy promotes data sovereignty and the establishment of European data spaces, enabling secure and controlled data sharing across sectors. For education, this aligns with efforts to establish interoperable credentialing systems that respect data protection and promote cross-border recognition of qualifications.

**Strategic priorities:**
- European data sovereignty
- Sectoral data spaces
- Secure data sharing protocols
- Cross-border data flows

**Educational implications:**
- Supports the data protection measures in [Chapter 6](chapter6.md)
- Enables the interoperability requirements detailed in [Chapter 8](chapter8.md)
- Underpins the privacy-preserving architecture throughout the framework

These regulatory foundations provide the legal, technical, and strategic context that enables the comprehensive digital credential framework described throughout this document. The alignment between regulatory requirements and framework capabilities ensures both legal compliance and practical effectiveness in supporting educational and professional mobility across Europe.
