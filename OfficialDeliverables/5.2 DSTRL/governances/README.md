# European Governance in Education and Professional Qualifications

## Introduction

This document provides a comprehensive overview of how education and professional qualifications are governed across the European Union, examining the multi-level governance structures that operate from European to national and regional levels. The governance framework reflects the principle of subsidiarity whilst enabling cross-border recognition and mobility through coordinated European frameworks.

The European approach to education and professional qualifications governance is characterised by **national competence with European coordination**, where Member States retain sovereignty over their education systems whilst participating in shared frameworks that facilitate mutual recognition and transparency.

## 1. Formal Education Governance

### European Level Coordination

Formal education governance in the EU operates on the fundamental principle that **education is a national competence**. There is no single European authority with legal powers over national education systems. Instead, the European Union provides coordination mechanisms and frameworks that enable harmonisation whilst respecting national autonomy.

#### Key European Coordination Tools

**European Qualifications Framework (EQF)**
- Provides a common reference framework that enables comparison of qualifications across Member States
- Aligns national qualifications to ensure comparability and transparency
- Maps qualifications from EQF Level 1 (basic general knowledge) to EQF Level 8 (doctoral level)

**European Education Area (EEA)**
- Strategic framework fostering cooperation to enhance education quality and mobility
- Promotes mutual learning and policy coordination among Member States
- Supports initiatives for digital transformation and green transition in education

**European Higher Education Area (EHEA)**
- Supports the Bologna Process for harmonised higher education standards
- Facilitates student and academic mobility through common degree structures
- Promotes quality assurance and recognition mechanisms

**Lisbon Recognition Convention**
- Legal framework enabling mutual recognition of higher education qualifications
- Supports fair recognition procedures and removal of obstacles to mobility
- Implemented at national level but coordinated at European level

**European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training (Cedefop)**
- Union agency established by Regulation (EU) 2019/128
- Based in Thessaloniki with liaison office in Brussels
- Supports VET, skills, and qualifications policies through research and analysis

### Member State Implementation

Within this European framework, Member States organise their education governance through multi-level delegation models:

**Central Public Authorities (Ministries)**
- Recognise and accredit educational institutions across all levels
- Define correspondence between national qualifications and EQF levels
- Grant operating licences and degree-awarding powers
- May distribute responsibilities across different ministries by education level

**Recognised Educational Institutions**
- Schools, colleges, universities, and training centres
- Must hold valid mandates to operate and issue official qualifications
- Must comply with both national legislation and European frameworks
- Include pre-school, primary, secondary, vocational, higher education, and adult education institutions

**Sectoral Bodies (where applicable)**
- May participate in curricula approval, particularly in vocational education
- Represent employer and employee interests in education governance
- Contribute to skills needs assessment and programme design

### Vocational Education and Training (VET) Specific Governance

VET governance exhibits particular characteristics due to its close connection to labour market needs:

**European Level**
- **Copenhagen Process**: Voluntary cooperation framework for VET policy
- **EQAVET**: Quality assurance framework providing guidelines and indicators
- **Europass**: Transparency tools for skills and qualifications
- **European Skills Agenda**: Strategic approach to skills development

**Cedefop's Role**
Cedefop operates with a tripartite governance structure involving:
- Representatives from Member States
- Employers' organisations
- Employees' organisations
- European Commission

Key functions include:
- Providing comparative analyses of VET systems and labour market trends
- Supporting qualifications development and alignment with EQF
- Promoting validation of non-formal and informal learning
- Facilitating knowledge sharing among stakeholders
- Managing tools and datasets for VET and skills

**National Level**
- Member States determine accreditation approaches (providers, programmes, or both)
- Sectoral bodies and chambers often have supervisory or awarding roles
- Integration with formal education systems varies by country

## 2. Professional Qualifications Governance

### European Framework

Professional qualifications governance follows the same principle of national competence with European coordination. The EU does not centrally regulate professions but provides frameworks for mutual recognition and mobility.

#### Key European Instruments

**Professional Qualifications Directive (2005/36/EC, amended by 2013/55/EU)**
- Legal framework for recognition of professional qualifications
- Establishes principles for automatic recognition for specific professions
- Provides general system for recognition of qualifications
- Includes safeguards for public health and safety

**Internal Market Information System (IMI)**
- Electronic system enabling cooperation between competent authorities
- Facilitates information exchange on professional qualifications
- Supports verification of authenticity and validity of qualifications
- Enables alert mechanisms for professional misconduct

**European Professional Card (EPC)**
- Electronic certificate for specific professions
- Streamlines recognition procedures
- Currently available for nurses, pharmacists, physiotherapists, real estate agents, and mountain guides

### Member State Organisation

Professional qualifications governance typically follows this structure:

**Ministries or Central Public Authorities**
- Define which professions are regulated within national jurisdiction
- Establish regulatory frameworks for professional practice
- Authorise National Professional Bodies to oversee specific professions
- Ensure compliance with EU recognition principles

**National Professional Bodies (NPBs)**
- Legally authorised entities responsible for professional regulation
- Functions include:
  - Issuing professional licences and certificates
  - Maintaining professional registers and databases
  - Overseeing continuing professional development requirements
  - Representing professions in national and European forums
  - Ensuring ethical standards and professional conduct

**Regulated Professions**
- Professions where practice is subject to legal requirements
- Common examples include healthcare, legal, engineering, teaching, and architecture
- Requirements typically include specific education, examinations, and ongoing registration

### Cross-border Recognition Mechanisms

**Automatic Recognition**
- Applies to specific professions with harmonised minimum training requirements
- Includes doctors, nurses, dentists, veterinarians, pharmacists, midwives, and architects
- Based on mutual trust in education and training standards

**General System of Recognition**
- Applies to professions not covered by automatic recognition
- Based on comparison of education and training levels
- May require compensation measures (adaptation period or aptitude test)

**Recognition on the Basis of Professional Experience**
- For certain craft, industrial, and commercial activities
- Based on pursuit of activity for specific periods in other Member States

## 3. Quality Assurance Governance

Quality assurance governance varies between higher education and vocational education and training, reflecting different organisational structures and needs.

### Higher Education Quality Assurance

#### European Level Coordination

**European Association for Quality Assurance in Higher Education (ENQA)**
- Membership organisation for quality assurance agencies
- Develops and promotes European Standards and Guidelines (ESG)
- Facilitates cooperation and mutual learning among QA agencies

**European Quality Assurance Register for Higher Education (EQAR)**
- Register of quality assurance agencies that comply with ESG
- Enables transparency and trust in quality assurance across borders
- Supports agencies in operating beyond their home countries

**European Standards and Guidelines (ESG)**
- Common framework for quality assurance in higher education
- Cover internal quality assurance, external quality assurance, and quality assurance agencies
- Regularly updated through broad stakeholder consultation

#### National and Regional Implementation

**National Quality Assurance Agencies**
- Recognised by ministries of education or equivalent authorities
- Responsible for institutional and programme accreditation
- Must comply with ESG to be listed in EQAR
- Examples include ANECA (Spain), ANVUR (Italy), NVAO (Netherlands)

**Regional Quality Assurance Bodies**
- Operate under national authorisation in decentralised systems
- Conduct quality reviews within specific geographic areas
- Examples include AQU Catalunya in Spain's Catalonia region

**Institutional and Programme Level**
- Universities and higher education institutions subject to external review
- May receive accreditation at institutional level, programme level, or both
- Internal quality assurance systems required as part of ESG compliance

### Vocational Education and Training Quality Assurance

#### European Level Framework

**European Quality Assurance in Vocational Education and Training (EQAVET)**
- Voluntary framework providing guidelines and indicators
- Developed by Member States, social partners, and European Commission
- Does not accredit or certify - purely advisory and supportive role

**Quality Assurance Characteristics**
- More diverse arrangements compared to higher education
- No single register of accredited agencies
- National, regional, and sectoral variations in approach
- Integration of employer and employee perspectives

#### National and Regional Organisation

**National and Regional QA Agencies**
- Established according to national legislation and preferences
- May accredit providers, programmes, or both
- Often coordinate with sectoral bodies and chambers

**Sectoral Bodies and Chambers**
- Significant role in many Member States
- May have supervisory or awarding responsibilities
- Represent employer interests in quality assurance processes

**Awarding Bodies**
- Separate from training providers in many systems
- Responsible for assessment and certification
- Subject to approval by competent national authorities

## 4. Digital Identity Governance in Education and Professional Contexts

### Non-foundational Digital Identities

Digital identity governance in education and professional contexts involves credentials that are domain-specific rather than linked to civil registers.

#### Types of Digital Identities

**Educational ID**
- Domain-specific identifier for pupils, students, and learners
- Issued by schools, VET centres, and higher education institutions
- Enables identity matching and service access within education systems

**Professional ID**
- Identity credential for registered professionals in regulated sectors
- Issued by authorised professional bodies
- Facilitates verification of professional status and qualifications

**MyAcademicID**
- Federated student identity for academic services
- Coordinated through National Research and Education Networks (NRENs)
- Enables cross-border access to academic resources and services

**Alliance ID**
- Identity within European University Alliances
- Issued by participating universities for joint programmes and services
- Supports collaborative education initiatives

**European Student Card**
- Identity credential within European Student Card Initiative
- Facilitates student mobility and access to services
- Coordinated at European level with national implementation

#### Governance Structure

**European Level Coordination**
- **GEANT**: Coordinates cross-border student identity federations
- **eduGAIN**: Technical framework for identity federation interoperability
- **European Education Area**: Policy framework supporting digital identity initiatives

**National Research and Education Networks (NRENs)**
- Act as trusted identity providers for academic institutions
- Examples include RedIRIS (Spain), RENATER (France), GARR (Italy)
- Facilitate cross-border authentication and authorisation

**Institutional Level**
- Universities and educational institutions as identity issuers
- Professional bodies as issuers of professional identity credentials
- Must operate under appropriate mandates and authorisations

## 5. Cross-cutting Governance Principles

### Subsidiarity and National Competence

The governance of education and professional qualifications embodies the principle of subsidiarity, where:
- Member States retain primary responsibility for education policy and organisation
- Professional regulation remains a national competence
- European level provides coordination tools and frameworks
- Regional authorities may have significant roles in decentralised systems

### Mutual Recognition and Trust

European governance frameworks are built on principles of:
- **Mutual trust** in Member State education and professional systems
- **Transparency** through common frameworks and quality assurance
- **Proportionality** in recognition requirements and procedures
- **Non-discrimination** in access to education and professional opportunities

### Quality Assurance and Accountability

Quality assurance operates through:
- **European standards** providing common reference points
- **National implementation** respecting different traditions and structures
- **Peer review** and mutual learning among institutions and agencies
- **Stakeholder involvement** including students, employers, and civil society

### Digital Transformation

The governance framework increasingly incorporates:
- **Digital credentials** and identity management systems
- **Interoperability** standards for cross-border recognition
- **Privacy protection** in line with GDPR requirements
- **Accessibility** and inclusion in digital education services

## Conclusion

European governance in education and professional qualifications represents a sophisticated multi-level system that balances national sovereignty with European coordination. The framework enables mobility, recognition, and quality assurance whilst respecting the diversity of national systems and traditions.

This governance model provides the foundation for digital transformation initiatives that can enhance transparency, mobility, and trust across European education and professional systems. The challenge for implementation lies in maintaining this balance whilst leveraging new technologies to improve services for learners, professionals, and employers across the European Union.
